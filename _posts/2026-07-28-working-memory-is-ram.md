We also have something similar to "RAM": our short-term or working memory.
Like RAM, it's temporary and limited, but very powerful when we use it for
the right things. We're very good at making connections and generating new
ideas, but not so good at reliably storing and recalling small, isolated
pieces of information.

That means trying to "remember everything" — every meeting detail, every
pending task, every decision — isn't just exhausting: it's using the wrong
tool for the job. Working memory is meant to hold information just long
enough to do something with it: understand it, transform it, or move it to a
better place.

That "better place" is whatever external system you choose: a notebook, a
task manager, meeting notes. The moment something leaves your head and
enters a system you trust, it stops occupying RAM. That frees up space for
what working memory does best: connecting ideas, not storing them.

The result of using this layer well is a more efficient use of your mental
"storage," less wasted effort, and less mental energy needed to perform at
your best.
