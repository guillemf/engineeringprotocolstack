A group of people sharing a project isn't automatically a team. The
difference shows up when there are clear roles, shared processes, and
something like a common identity: a "north star" everyone understands the
same way.

In the **LAN** layer of the book — the layer of your team — the starting
point is honestly identifying what state your team is in today. Not every
team needs the same kind of intervention: a new team has different problems
than a team that's been working together for years but has lost cohesion
during a phase of rapid growth.

Some questions that help diagnose it:

- Do the people on the team understand their role, or are they improvising it?
- Is there a clear career path, or does growth depend on who shouts the loudest?
- Does the team have its own processes, or does it just inherit the organization's without questioning them?
- Is there a shared goal that everyone could explain in their own words?

None of these questions has a purely technical answer. That's why, for many
engineers moving into leadership, this layer is the most uncomfortable one:
it doesn't come with documentation, and the mistakes take weeks or months to
show up.
