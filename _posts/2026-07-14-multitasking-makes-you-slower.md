Every day we compete with too many things for our attention: messages,
meetings, ideas that pop up out of nowhere, urgent requests while we're
already busy with something else. If we want to do work that matters, we
need a simple way to decide what to do, what to postpone, and what to
ignore.

We often say we're "multitasking," but that's not really what's happening.
Most of the time we're simply moving our attention very quickly from one
task to another. It feels efficient, but in practice it usually slows us
down.

The reason is simple: every time we switch tasks we pay a mental cost. We
need a few moments to remember where we were, what we were thinking, and
what comes next. That cost may seem small, but repeated many times a day it
becomes expensive.

A simple example makes this clear: try stirring your coffee while reading a
paragraph carefully. At some point, one of the two actions slows down or
stops, because your attention has shifted to the other.

**The key idea:** the goal isn't to turn yourself into a machine that does
more and more. The goal is to reduce friction, make better decisions, and
spend your energy on the work that actually matters.
